\begin{abstract}
Large Concept Models (LCMs) operate over sentence-level semantic embeddings rather than token sequences, but their current encoders, particularly SONAR inherit the tokenization biases of BPE, which fragment morphologically complex words in agglutinative scripts. We propose BLT-LCM, a hybrid architecture that replaces SONAR with a Byte Latent Transformer (BLT) encoder whose entropy-based dynamic patch segmentation operates directly on raw bytes, eliminating tokenization bias from the concept encoding pipeline. For agglutinative languages, BLT's entropy signal naturally aligns patch boundaries with morpheme boundaries, producing concept representations that respect linguistic structure rather than vocabulary artifacts. We formalize this intuition as a bound on concept encoding error as a function of a language's morpheme-to-token fertility ratio. Empirically, we evaluate Marathi - a low-resource agglutinative language written in Devanagari script using the BhashaSetu corpus, training against SONAR-LCM and BPE-based baselines across three data regimes. BLT-LCM achieves consistent gains on BLEU, chrF, and COMET, with the largest improvements in the lowest-resource setting, consistent with our theoretical prediction. To our knowledge, this is the first contribution targeting the tokenization bottleneck in concept-level neural MT from non-Latin script languages
\end{abstract}
